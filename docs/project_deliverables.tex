\subsection*{Decisions and Verification}
\subsubsection*{LLM Usage}
I used a mixture of Agentic and Code focused LLM tools including Snowflake Cortex Code and Github Copilot.
The tools were used to trace calculations through the notebook, create helper functions, 
draft the report check uncertainty definitions and compare results with manufacturer 
information. AI assisted checks included independent recalculation of selected summaries and LaTeX compilation. 
All automated tests were created by the AI tools.  I worked by questioning the results and 
asking for further information and explanation when I did not understand the results.

\subsubsection*{Deliverable 1}
I chose subpixel checkerboard calibration with radial and tangential distortion, keeping images 
in consistent sensor coordinates and fixing the third radial coefficient. I rejected treating fit 
errors as a complete uncertainty budget or comparing physical focal length directly with Apple’s 
equivalent focal length. Existing tests check zero residuals for a straight grid, correction of synthetic 
distortion, image dimension ordering and reprojection error against known offsets. The notebook also compares 
original and corrected checkerboard straightness. 

\subsubsection*{Deliverable 2}
I used black subtracted RAW green samples and slanted edge MTF, avoiding demosaicing and gamma processing. 
I reported frequencies per original RAW pixel and selected the worst corner by its average across captures, 
not by averaging individual minima. Existing tests cover synthetic target selection, uneven illumination, missing targets, 
batch completeness, frequency scaling and worst corner aggregation. Recomputed summaries agreed with the saved results. 
Review established that pooled corner spread mixes spatial variation with repeatability.

\subsubsection*{Deliverable 3}
I chose paired frame variance to suppress common fixed pattern and used dark frame differences rather than the single frame 
spatial variance intercept for read noise. Gain and dark current came from linear fits. Checks included comparison with single 
frame results and conversion of the DNG vendor noise model into matching units. Review found that read noise uncertainty includes
 only gain fit error, dark pairs share frames, and unequal exposures can introduce additional noise. Dark signals were also 
 close to the black offset.

\subsubsection*{Deliverable 4}
I defined range using metadata saturation above black divided by the dark noise floor, with an explicit read only noise threshold. 
I rejected the saved zero uncertainty because it resulted from taking the standard deviation of one scalar. 
The report therefore labels the result provisional. 

\subsubsection*{Deliverable 5}
I chose per channel flat field correction, white balancing, capture averaging and a least squares color matrix evaluated 
against the specified ColorChecker reference under D50. Existing tests check masked patch statistics against known values.
Review found that the maximum error uncertainty incorrectly reused across patch spread; that error bar was rejected. 
Because the same patches determine and evaluate the matrix, the improvement is in sample rather than independent validation.

\subsection*{Deliverable 1: Geometry Intrinsics and Lens Distortion}

\noindent\textbf{Method.}
\JSONParseValue{\results}{n_calibration_images_used} DNG captures
are captured using the same rear camera and a $9\times6$ internal-corner checkerboard.
Corners were refined to subpixel precision on demosaiced, unrotated images;
\texttt{calibrateCameraExtended} determines the camera intrinsic parameters from the detected corners.
The distortion model uses radial terms $1+k_1r^2+k_2r^4$ in normalized
coordinates and tangential coefficients $p_1,p_2$; $k_3=0$ was fixed, and assumed to be negligible.
 Checkerboard spacing was measured in mm using a ruler.  A speculative
absolute uncertainty of 0.25 mm was used based on the precision of the ruler. Image dimensions
and capture counts are exact metadata/counts, not uncertain measurements.

\noindent\textbf{Calculations and uncertainty.}
Intrinsic uncertainties are OpenCV's approximate $1\sigma$ fit errors,
not repeat-calibration standard deviations or full systematic bounds.
The notebook averages the two focal lengths and propagates their errors:
\[
    \bar f=\frac{f_x+f_y}{2},\qquad
    u(\bar f)=\frac{\sqrt{u(f_x)^2+u(f_y)^2}}{2},\qquad
    f_{\rm mm}=p\bar f.
\]
The first propagation neglects axis covariance. The assumed
$p=0.002\,\mathrm{mm/px}$ has no verified tolerance; hence the physical
focal-length uncertainty is \emph{fit-only}, conditional on that scale.
If pitch uncertainty were known, independent propagation would give
$u(f_{\rm mm})^2=p^2u(\bar f)^2+\bar f^2u(p)^2$.
Reprojection RMS pools squared Euclidean corner residuals over all views;
its accompanying spread is \texttt{np.std(errorValues)} (population
standard deviation of per-view RMS), not a confidence interval on the
pooled RMS. The notebook's before/after straightness check is in-sample,
not independent validation.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Manufacturer comparison.}
\href{https://support.apple.com/en-us/121029}{Apple's iPhone 16 specification}
lists a nominal $26\,\mathrm{mm}$ equivalent main lens, not its physical
focal length, and gives no tolerance. Matching image diagonals to the
$36\times24\,\mathrm{mm}$ reference format gives
$f_{\rm eq}=\bar f\sqrt{36^2+24^2}/\sqrt{4032^2+3024^2}
=(26.83\pm0.04)\,\mathrm{mm}$ (fit-only $1\sigma$),
or $(3.19\pm0.16)\%$ above that nominal value.
RAW versus advertised
field-of-view/crop conventions, focus changes and calibration-model bias
are possible contributors, but were not isolated or further tested.

\subsection*{Deliverable 2: Spatial Resolution MTF}

\noindent\textbf{Method.}
Five DNG captures supplied black-subtracted samples from one Bayer green
plane, without demosaicing or gamma encoding. Near-vertical target edges
were selected at the center and four corners. Samples were aligned by
perpendicular distance to a fitted edge and binned into a four-times-oversampled
edge-spread function (ESF). Differentiation gives the line-spread function
(LSF); its Hann-windowed Fourier magnitude is normalized at zero frequency:
\[
    \mathrm{MTF}(f)=\frac{|\mathcal{F}\{w\,\mathrm{LSF}\}(f)|}
    {|\mathcal{F}\{w\,\mathrm{LSF}\}(0)|},\qquad
    \mathrm{MTF}(f_{50})=0.5.
\]
The first downward half-response crossing is linearly interpolated.
Green-plane spacing is twice the original RAW pixel pitch, so its axial
Nyquist limit is $0.25$ cycles/original pixel. The pitch assumed for intermediate physical units cancels
when converting back to cycles/original pixel.

\noindent\textbf{Uncertainty and interpretation.}
The reported uncertainties are population standard deviations.
Center and worst-corner spreads each use five captures;
the corner mean pools twenty values, mixing spatial variation with
capture repeatability. The top-right corner has the lowest five-capture
mean.
Corners resolve less detail than the center in the tested edge direction.
These are target-plus-camera green-channel responses, not isolated lens
MTF; printed-edge quality, alignment, aliasing and estimator bias are not
bounded by the reported scatter.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Manufacturer comparison.}
\href{https://support.apple.com/en-us/121029}{Apple's published specification}
provides no comparable MTF50 figure. Its nominal megapixel count describes
sampling, not contrast transfer, so it cannot establish agreement with
these RAW edge measurements.

\subsection*{Deliverable 3: Noise}

\noindent\textbf{Method.}
At reference-capture ISO \JSONParseValue{\results}{iso_of_noise_measurement},
central $128\times128$ green-sample patches from eleven flat-field pairs
provided black-subtracted mean $\mu$ and temporal variance
$v=\operatorname{Var}(A-B)/2$.
Five levels within the selected linear range were fitted to $v=g\mu+b$;
$g$ is conversion gain in DN/electron. Differencing suppresses common fixed
pattern. Five dark captures supplied ten overlapping pairs, giving
$\sigma_{\rm DN}=\sqrt{\langle v_{\rm dark}\rangle}$ and
$\sigma_e=\sigma_{\rm DN}/g$. Dark current is the slope of mean
black-subtracted dark signal in electrons versus exposure time.

\noindent\textbf{Uncertainty and comparison.}
Gain and dark-current uncertainties are $1\sigma$ slope standard errors
from fit covariance; dark-current error holds gain fixed. Read-noise error
is only $u(\sigma_e)=\sigma_e u(g)/g$, omitting uncertainty in the dark
variance. Shared frames make the ten pairs non-independent; unequal
exposures also admit dark-current shot noise and exposure-dependent
pattern. Dark means lie close to the black offset, so the current slope
is provisional and black-level drift is not bounded.

The DNG vendor \texttt{NoiseProfile}, interpreted as normalized variance
$a x+b_v$, predicts $g_v=aS\simeq0.344$ DN/$e^-$ and
$\sigma_{e,v}=\sqrt{b_vS^2}/g_v\simeq10.26\,e^-$, where $S$ is the
metadata white-minus-black range. These are nominal model predictions
without supplied tolerances, both higher than the fitted results.
The discrepancy may reflect central-patch selection, the restricted
signal range or dark-frame conditions, but vendor characterization
conditions are unspecified and the cause cannot be isolated.

\subsection*{Deliverable 4: Dynamic Range}

\noindent\textbf{Calculation and uncertainty.}
Using metadata white level
$W=\JSONParseValue{\results}{white_level_dn}$ DN and black level
$B=\num[round-precision=0]{\JSONParseExpandableValue{\results}{black_level_dn[0]}}$ DN,
the notebook defines $S=W-B$ and $D=\log_2(S/\sigma_{\rm DN})$.
These code levels are metadata, not independently measured saturation or
black-offset calibrations. The low end is one RMS dark-noise level:
SNR unity in a read-noise-only convention, not exactly unity when shot
noise is included.

\subsection*{Deliverable 5: Color Accuracy}

\noindent\textbf{Method.}
Three chart captures were black-subtracted, normalized and per-channel
flat-field corrected, then their linear RGB patch means were averaged.
White patch 19 supplied white-balance gains. The baseline treats balanced
camera RGB as linear sRGB with Bradford adaptation to D50; it is not
Apple's rendered color pipeline. A least-squares $3\times3$ matrix maps
balanced camera RGB to reference XYZ. Both results are converted to Lab
under D50 and evaluated using CIEDE2000 ($\Delta E_{00}$), against
\emph{ColorChecker24 -- After November 2014}.
